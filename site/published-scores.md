# Fude: which songs play from a published score

With **As written** chosen (the default), a library song that has a published score plays every part of that score: both hands of a piano piece, all four voices of a hymn, every string or orchestral part. The score pane in the full-window view engraves exactly what plays. The other songs play Fude's own arrangement of the checked tune. Fude says which, under the player and above the sheet music.

Every score below comes from the Mutopia Project (LilyPond sources, compiled to MIDI with repeats unfolded). Each was checked note by note against the tune Fude had already checked: the right notes at the right times, allowing for a different key or note values.

Scores play at Fude's checked tempo. Where a hymnal prints a tune in another key, the hymnal's key is used.

## Tempo and dynamics as printed
Each score shows its printed tempo word with the metronome mark it plays at, counted in the beat its meter uses. The dynamics the score prints are engraved under the top staff, set how loudly each note is played, and shade the painting: a region painted while the music is loud is darker, and one painted while it is soft is lighter. Scores that print no dynamics, such as the hymnals and the Bach, play at an even level.

## Why some have none
- Mutopia is the only large collection of public-domain engraved scores this build could reach. It has no settings of most children's songs, folk songs or film-era pieces.
- Morning Mood, the Swan Lake theme, Also sprach Zarathustra and the William Tell overture are orchestral works with no reachable score. Zarathustra is still in copyright in many countries.
- Auld Lang Syne, Au clair de la lune and Twinkle Twinkle exist only as guitar arrangements there, so they were not used. Ode to Joy exists only as a hymnal setting, not Beethoven's symphony, so it was not used either.

## Rebuilding
`scores/` in the build folder holds each downloaded `.ly`, the compiled `.mid`, `found-*.json` (where each score came from and which beats match), `overrides.json` (display wording, pickups and rejections) and `build_scores.py`, which writes `library-scores.json` for the page.
